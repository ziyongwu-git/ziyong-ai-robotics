In November 2026 I shipped a multi-school web app for dance-school-style programs: classes, attendance, invoices, and payments. The first version was ClassPortal. On November 22 I renamed it ClassLista. The Chinese name is 课堂通. I built it with AI coding agents.

The live app is [classlista.com](https://classlista.com).

## Who it is for

Three roles use it: a website admin, school admins and teachers, and families.

## What it does

Through November 11, a session can be accepted or declined, and the confirmation shows on a calendar. Attendance gets taken. Invoice emails go out.

Invites and password resets are email too. Email goes through Resend or SMTP. Each school sets the sender name and the reply-to on those messages.

The language toggle is English, Chinese, and Spanish. A test fails when a translation is missing. The design is kid-friendly, and the tooltips are accessible.

## Ask ClassPortal

The assistant is called Ask ClassPortal. Grok is the default. OpenAI or Anthropic can be used instead. It is there for sales and for guidance.

"Take me there" highlights the field you asked about. Lookups are read-only, and they stay inside the role and the school of the person asking. There is a setup checklist, and a place to capture a lead.

I put limits on it. Requests are rate-limited. Chats are kept for 30 days, and there is an admin page for them. If no API key is set, it searches the help docs instead of calling a model.

## How it is put together

The front of it is Next.js 15, React 19, and TypeScript, styled with Tailwind v4. Data is Prisma 6 on SQLite. Sign-in is a custom session: bcrypt for passwords, hashed tokens for the session. Changes go through server actions checked with zod 4, and queries are scoped to the role and the school.

It runs on Railway, in Docker, on Node 22, with a persistent volume. Platform email sends through Resend.

Tests run with Node's built-in test runner, via tsx. GitHub Actions runs CI. ESLint, Prettier, and Dependabot are part of the setup.

## November 11 to 23

After the first ship I kept going on the same app, through November 23. The work below is grouped by theme.

### Name and home

On November 22 I renamed ClassPortal to ClassLista. The Chinese name is 课堂通. The public address is classlista.com. [www.classlista.com](https://www.classlista.com) and the old Railway address both redirect there. Platform email now actually sends, through Resend, from its own mail domain.

### Teachers and attendance

Attendance is Present, Absent, or Excused. The teacher Today screen takes attendance in one tap, and that attendance can be edited for 30 days. A corrected class adjusts the invoice with a credit or a charge. Teachers have self-serve powers, and an admin can switch those powers on or off.

### Families

A school has a join code. The code is random, and there is no public school directory. Class emails include Coming and Can't make it. Those are one tap, with no sign-in. A family can say yes to the rest of the term. Make-up classes are opt-in, per school. The join page shows where a family stands.

### Scheduling

A term has a roster. A class can have several weekly times, and it can repeat until a date. This class and the following ones can be edited or cancelled. The app warns about a clash for the same teacher, the same room, or the same child. Staff Planner can be viewed by teacher, room, student, or family. Holidays can start from suggested days, and a school can add custom days off, with conflict warnings and reminders. Places have their own time zones. On a phone, the calendar opens as an upcoming list.

### Money

Invoices show Paid, Unpaid, or Overdue, and staff record payments. A school lists how families pay: Zelle, Venmo, PayPal, a card link to the school's own Stripe, Square, or PayPal page, bank transfer, check, cash, WeChat Pay, Alipay, or a custom method. A family taps "I've paid", and staff confirm. The app keeps payment records and refund records. ClassLista itself does not move the money yet.

### Owner view

The owner view has a platform home, school pages, a usage dashboard, and pages for emails, activity, the system, and people. Open as admin is a support mode, and it is time-limited.

### Look and feel

There is a public landing page, with a language picker. Notices, dates, and amounts follow the reader's language. The new look came in seven passes. It includes school colors and a logo, a floating tab bar, drawn status icons, and warmer neutrals. The fonts are Bricolage Grotesque and Figtree. Each role has a phone-first home. I polished accessibility and the phone layout. There is an app icon, and links have previews. Setup cards open a school one step at a time.

### Hardening

CI runs on every pull request. Login is throttled, and an account can lock. A person can sign out of all devices. Sessions end when the password changes. I closed two screens that showed data across schools. The database is copied before a migration. Content-Security-Policy is on in report-only mode. There is a health page. Dependencies were upgraded.

### Ask ClassPortal, continued

How-to answers are grounded in the step-by-step help guides. Suggest a feature sends that feedback to the website admin.

## What I learned

I got to take a product the whole way: scheduling, attendance, invoices, payments, email, languages, and a live URL. Ask ClassPortal is the applied LLM piece, and the guardrails are part of the product: rate limits, 30 days of chat, an admin page, read-only lookups scoped to a role and a school, and help-doc search when no API key is set. More than one school means the data has to say which school a row belongs to. Three languages count because a missing translation fails a test. I shipped it with AI coding agents.

After November 11 the cadence stayed fast and agent-driven, in dozens of small pull requests. I kept that pace in bounds with CI on every pull request, the hardening above, and a copy of the database before a migration.
